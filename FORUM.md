## M480 Pad

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Dec 21, 2012|45_

> Well as evolution did tis thing the LM201 became the LM301. The LM301 underwent a few changes over the years, and both components were in production by National Semi-Conductor, and Texas Instruments (maybe 1or 2 others as well) for many years. We used both components interchangeably due to availability. But he "newer" LM301 became the more common component going forward in our production cycles. IF you have a module with an LM201 and you drop in an LM301 that you bought lets say today, you will not only measure a difference, I would argue that in a quiet studio, you will be able to hear the difference (listen to the thermal noise generated by the unit when on). I feel that on my first T1202A I could hear it on the M480B modules, I believe I am able to hear it on my M460 modules from the 70's and even the ones ion the 80's. AND to further this - I had a sick M402B a few years ago. dad and I opened it up and found the bad op-amp - an LM201. It really failed. On the scope the noise was a consistent rumble, ever present and very obvious when compared to the modules twin. So we pulled the IC, which was split on the EQ's PCB, but still working! Dad dropped in a new LM301, and of course the EQ worked and behaved quite nicely. Then we compared it to the twin 402 eq. Dad's eyebrows shot up, and he replaced the op-amps in that EQ as well. For fun I am refurbishing 20 odd M460 modules for my console, but leaving 2 modules 100% un-touched. When this project is done, I will explore the differences in 4-109/8-109 cards, and carious voltage regulator and op-amp configurations. 
>
> But yes, I believe the change form the old LM301 to modern day LM301's will net you a small, measurable change. And if you ran all modules open - the noise floor as a whole, will be measurably lower.
>
> Regards,
>
> Warren

## No stereo signal out of the L1202

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Jun 26, 2013|46_

> Well a L1202 (T1202??)is really a dual mono board - for sterio you will have to visit the world's first true sterio consoles the ST series that we made/invented. 
>
> But that is semantics. You should be sending signal out of M1 and M2 if all the wiring is complete, if the faders are hooked in place, if the summing modules are not damaged, the assignment module(s - depending upon the version) is assigning the audio in the correct manner to your monitoring out-put, and lastly you must assign the input channel to either M1 or M2 to thus route the signal to an output. If you have the M480C module the pan control should be in the centre position, and if you are sending signal in to the little console on input A make certain that you have activated the appropriate channel, and have selected input A - as each module M480, M480A, M480B, and M480C all have 2 inputs per module.
>
> Also Tony has scanned and posted many manuals here on the WBSPS, and I believe there is a T1202 manual there and a couple of L series as well, to help you with signal routing, etc...
>
> Regards,
